The problem

You don't stop after every song to reconfigure your speakers.

So why does every game switch mean browsing a library, re-inviting everyone, remapping controllers, sitting through intros and loading screens? GameNight deletes all of it. The whole evening becomes one continuous multiplayer session.

How it feels

Walk in. Grab a controller. Press A.

🛋️

The party is persistent

People, seats and names live above the games. Player 2 stays player 2 all night — across every game. No accounts, no lobbies, no setup.

✨

The next game is already warm

While you play, the next game silently loads behind the current one. Skipping a game is as instant as skipping a track.

🗳️

Voting is gameplay

Match over? No dialog boxes. Everyone stands their avatar on Replay, Next game or Quit — consensus starts the transition.

Under the hood

A daemon, a protocol, and games that just show up.

A small Rust daemon hosts the party and launches games on demand. Games speak a seven-message WebSocket protocol — open by design, with a conformance harness, SDKs for Godot and Rust, and a 60-line no-dependency example. If your favourite couch game isn't integrated yet, it can be — in under an hour.

Character Studio & Game Store

Design your character. Scan QR. Play together.

GameNight includes a built-in Pixel Art Character Designer and Game Store. Players scan the on-screen QR code on their phones, create custom avatars, buy games, and share their libraries in one unified party session.

Both live inside GameNight itself, on the machine hosting the party — your phone pairs with the couch over your own network, so there is nothing to sign into and no account to lose.
